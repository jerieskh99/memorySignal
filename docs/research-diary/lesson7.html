Building an instrument worth trusting

A hundred and one workloads across thirteen classes of computation, a retention scheme that keeps every raw snapshot at a two-hundredth of the size, and a written-down prediction about what the signal will fail to distinguish — made before the measurement, so it can be wrong.

The one idea to hold onto

Roughly half of all computation is predicted to look, to this instrument, exactly like a program sweeping through memory. Writing that down in advance converts a limitation into a testable claim.

1
The reframe of the reframe

The negative was a property of the question

Entry 9 concluded "characterisation, not detection", and that the two memory twins could not be separated. A review of that conclusion found it had understated its own evidence.

The same committed results file already contained a novelty detector scoring 0.925 with an entire unseen family held out. And the two "indistinguishable" workloads are, in the raw signal, not overlapping at all — separated by an effect size of roughly 5.6 standard deviations. They scored zero because a supervised classifier was asked to separate them, and it had no example of one of them to learn from.

Takeaway

The failure was in how the question was posed, not in the signal. Ask "which of these known classes is this?" and an unseen class scores zero by construction. Ask "does this look like anything I have seen before?" and the same data answers well.

The whole plan was reorganised around a per-family behaviour recogniser, with threat-flagging demoted to a downstream corollary. Before anything else was touched, the old analysis was re-run as a regression anchor — and it reproduced, slightly beating the quoted numbers at 0.9417 and 0.9593.

It also reproduced two warnings

Family identification on an unseen workload collapsed to 0.3485, below its own baseline; and recall on the held-out threat family was 0.25. Recorded at the time as "an early warning that the four ransomware simulations may not actually look alike in memory" — which is a question about the corpus, not the method.

A failure mode named before it happened

A rule that clears anything matching a known-benign family would launder the stealth workload: it is quiet, so in a magnitude-driven space its nearest neighbour is benign. The guard written in advance — run on magnitude-invariant features, and keep that workload's recall on the headline table so it can never hide inside an average.

2
The corpus

Thirteen kinds of computation

Six workloads (Entry 4) became eleven (Entry 7) became a hundred and one. But the interesting change is not the count — it is that the corpus acquired a second, orthogonal way of being organised.

The first division is by memory signature: idle, memory-intensive, disk, threaded, bulk-rewrite, enumeration, stealth, application-like, mixed. The second is by what kind of computation the program performs, borrowed from a published taxonomy of thirteen algorithmic motifs — dense and sparse linear algebra, spectral methods, n-body, structured and unstructured grids, Monte Carlo, combinational logic, graph traversal, dynamic programming, backtracking, graphical models, finite state machines.

test_families_spec.md · why this taxonomy

A motif of this kind "captures a pattern of computation and communication — largely a memory-access pattern, which is what the host memory signal sees."

It also answers a reviewer's question the first corpus could not: is this a representative spread of programs, or just the ones that were convenient to write?

Every workload carries both labels, and every motif is additionally graded by write-visibility — from clearly visible down to quiet: read- or compute-bound, and therefore nearly invisible. Those quiet ones are not omissions. They are the control group for the question Entry 4 raised and never closed: is this kind of computation invisible to memory introspection?

The cleanest experiment in the corpus

Two workloads run the same search over the same problem. One only counts the solutions; the other stores them. Nothing differs but the return type — and that alone should flip the workload from invisible to visible. The same trick is repeated with a pattern matcher that recognises versus one that emits output. The variable being tested is isolated almost perfectly.

On rigour and on safety

Every kernel is checked against an independent reference implementation — a factorisation against its own reconstruction, a fast transform against a round trip, an approximate n-body against direct summation, one shortest-path algorithm against another. The smoke battery climbed from 196 to 292 passing checks with zero failures. And a network family was deliberately not built: keeping zero networking code in the tree is a standing safety guarantee, so every "threat" is a reversible transformation on disposable files.

The thesis contribution

Predicting your own confusion

Sorting workloads by where they came from produced a problem: 59 of the 64 computation kernels landed in a single bucket called "kernel". That bucket is a statement about provenance, not about signal — and their write behaviour genuinely differs.

The instrument does not see "which algorithm". It sees a write shape. So each kernel was tagged, before capture, with the archetype it is predicted to be confused with:

The decision was to keep the provenance grouping and put the prediction beside it as a separate column, explicitly marked provisional. Four calls were flagged in advance as genuinely uncertain — a transform that is dense in structure but scattered in access, a tree-rebuilding algorithm that is both scatter and growth, and one whose content may resemble encryption rather than computation.

Why this is the contribution

"Our method cannot distinguish these" is a limitation. "We predict, in advance, exactly which of these it cannot distinguish, and here is the measured confusion matrix" is a result. The predictions can be wrong — and two already are. One kernel labelled near-idle turned out to be among the heaviest in the corpus; another labelled clearly visible turned out to be the quietest.

4
The retention answer

Keeping everything, affordably

Entry 6 killed raw retention on arithmetic: even well compressed, the archive ran forty times over budget. The fix was to stop compressing snapshots individually. Consecutive memory images are nearly identical, so each is stored as a patch against the one before it — a chain, with only the first stored in full.

Two approaches were built and measured against real captured data. A deduplicating backup tool came out at about 2.5× the theoretical ideal, even tuned to page-sized chunks. Patch chaining came out at 0.97× ideal — essentially optimal. Verified end to end: a forty-snapshot run compressed to about a two-hundredth of its raw size, and the rebuilt images matched byte for byte after transfer to another machine.

The physics lesson hiding in the calibration

The patch tool diffs bytes, while the signal counts pages. A workload writing one byte per page produces maximum measured activity and a tiny 1.7 MB patch. An encryptor producing incompressible output writes 287 MB per snapshot. Activity-heavy and storage-heavy are unrelated — and the snapshot rate turns out to be a clean linear function of patch size.

A design review caught a data-loss bug before it ran. Chain folders were named by workload and run, but the plan runs each workload twice with identical arguments — so both repeats collided in one folder, the tool refused to overwrite, and the second repeat's raw images would never have been deleted. On a long step that is roughly 600 GB on the system disk. Fixed with a four-level folder tree before any of it happened.

Designed, not built

The retention makes a richer measurement possible without recapturing — but the richer measurement itself is only partly built. A 64-column per-page feature set exists in working code. Two further families are specified and deliberately deferred, because they need something the pipeline cannot currently do: keep a reference — a pinned baseline, a running average, or a buffer of earlier snapshots — where today the previous image is deleted immediately.

Also specified and never emitted: magnitude and content entropy. Today's signal measures only breadth — how many pages moved — and is blind to how hard each one changed. That is precisely where a stealth encryptor hides: few pages touched, each completely rewritten. The per-page maximum is designed to catch it. No capture to date carries it.

A judgement call worth recording

Step notifications were going to be sent through a popular messaging service — until the realisation that the capture server belongs to a security research department and is likely monitored, and that regular-interval encrypted posts to that particular domain, timed to workload execution, is a textbook description of malware beaconing. Switched to ordinary mail through the server's own local relay, which is unremarkable traffic.

5
The campaign

Nine bugs between a plan and a result

The steps file was verified exhaustively first: all 94 binaries built clean, every flag cross-checked by a parser, all 101 lines dry-run end to end. "Workloads flagged for human review: None. Nothing was guessed."

Then it met a real guest, and nine things went wrong that no amount of checking the commands could have caught:

#What happened
1Nine steps asked for more memory than the guest has. Three failed outright; the other six ran, and measured swap thrashing instead of their access pattern.
2A memory-cap flag defaulted to 8 GB and was passed to none of the 69 workloads that accept it. Six sized their buffers from that default and tried to map 8 GB on a 1 GB guest.
3Scratch was on a RAM-backed filesystem. Large writes failed — and worse, the ones that succeeded registered as memory activity, so the disk workloads were silently measuring memory.
4A failed VM start was never checked, and the wait timeout defaulted to about fourteen days. An overnight run simply hung at step 18.
5Only the next cell's scratch was cleared, so every previous cell's output accumulated until the guest disk filled.
6Each step's final snapshot was archived but never deleted — about 101 GB across a campaign, against 192 GB free.
7The guest's shell treats a pattern matching nothing as a fatal error, so the cleanup command silently did nothing on every cell without hidden files.
8Cleanup used elevated privileges on paths that never needed them; the restricted policy denied it, and the script aborted.
9A workload launched over SSH returns an error if it lands in the window where the machine is paused for a snapshot. Seven of 101 steps failed this way — all tiny, all having passed every check.
The most insidious one

Number 3 deserves its own paragraph. The failures it caused loudly were annoying. The failures it caused quietly were dangerous: a disk-reading workload showed nearly ten times the expected activity, because its file writes were landing in memory. Valid-looking data with the wrong physics. And this exact bug had been found and fixed in June, in a different orchestrator — the fix was simply never inherited.

Enlarging the guest to solve the memory problems was considered and rejected for a reason worth keeping: it would have changed the denominator of the signal partway through the campaign, making the earlier and later cells incomparable.

Takeaway

The campaign completed on 10 August 2026: 100 workload signal chains captured losslessly, at correct sizing, on a real disk. Every one of the nine failures was a property of the environment, not of the commands — which is exactly what the next thing built was designed to catch.

6
The gate, and its own bugs

The check that lied about a hundred workloads

The response to nine bugs was a preflight gate that runs before a campaign and refuses it, built on a specific claim: every failure in the failed run was detectable beforehand, given the guest's memory and filesystem facts. Calibrated against that run, it flags all nine that failed — plus four more that completed while writing into the RAM-backed filesystem, and whose data was therefore invalid.

Its thresholds come from observation rather than theory. On the 964 MB guest: 384 MB runs fine, 512 MB fine, 1024 MB swaps heavily, 2048 MB fails outright. The reserve is set at 300 MB because the machine is rebooted before every step, so each workload meets a freshly booted system.

And then the checker had four bugs of its own, three of which gave confident wrong verdicts about healthy data:

False pass

One of 101

A helper read from standard input, consuming the loop's remaining list. It tested a single workload and reported "all 1 workloads exited 0" as a full pass.

Wrong subject

Measuring the wrapper

The memory probe captured the shell's process ID rather than the workload's, so every reading came back at about 1 MB.

Wrong rule

A correct failure that wasn't

It failed a workload for exceeding its memory budget. But that workload maps a file, and file-backed pages can be evicted. The check was wrong.

commit b072190

"A QA tool reporting a false pass is worse than no QA tool."

The fourth recurrence of this diary's most persistent pattern — after Entry 5's two broken probes, Entry 6's cell that failed for succeeding quietly, and Entry 8's schema mismatch that faked 132 passes.

The honest nuance on the third one

The workload it wrongly failed had died during the campaign — but on the RAM-backed filesystem, where no page can be evicted. The check was wrong about the rule and accidentally right about the outcome, for a reason that no longer applies.

Two more capture defects were fixed in the final week. Some workloads finish their work in seconds and idle out the rest of their window — the same "duration is a cap" bug from Entry 9, in a family that had never been checked for it, yielding as few as two snapshots. And heavy workloads were being throttled by a full processing queue while the guest kept running, burning their time budget and producing snapshots at irregular intervals — which breaks the even-spacing assumption every frequency measure depends on. The producer now freezes the machine while it waits, so the workload's clock stops with it.

And one obstacle solved without root

Memory copies started failing with a permission error on every snapshot: the security profile generated for this virtual machine does not list the dump directory, and it is regenerated on every start — which is why an earlier hands-on fix had reverted. The durable fix needs administrator access on a shared server, which is not available. The workaround uses a directory the profile does already permit, resolved fresh at each start because its name changes every boot.

Where it stands

The first experiment on the new instrument

Chosen on 29 August 2026: a characterisation run testing the predicted-confusion claim directly. Seventeen workloads, three repeats, fifty-one cells, about eight hours and five megabytes. Three anchors — one per archetype the kernels are predicted to collapse into — and fourteen kernels spread across all five, deliberately including both predictions already known to be wrong.

The design rationale, kept explicitly: a separability claim needs a variance decomposition. Repeats establish the noise floor; more than one workload per family establishes cohesion; only then does between-family distance mean anything. "One trace per family" was proposed and rejected for giving the last of those three and none of the others.

Two things to watch

The word "family" means two different things in this project — a filename prefix in the run planner, and a signature class in the specification — and they do not line up. And the planner emits cells in a fixed order, which over a long run aligns family with wall-clock drift: exactly the confound that the shuffling in the larger campaign generator was built to break.

What ten months bought

Not a detector. An instrument whose failure modes are known, whose blind spots are written down in advance, and whose most confident early results have already been taken apart by their own author. The measurements that survived did so because someone kept asking what a number was actually made of.

The analysis for this campaign does not exist yet. That is the next entry.

Glossary

Hover any dotted term for its definition.

Algorithmic motif
A recurring pattern of computation and memory access — dense linear algebra, graph traversal, spectral methods. Borrowed from a published taxonomy of thirteen, because the pattern is largely a memory-access pattern, which is what this instrument sees.
Write shape
What the instrument actually perceives: not which algorithm ran, but the geometry of which pages got written and how. Two unrelated algorithms with the same write shape are indistinguishable here.
Preflight gate
A check that runs before a campaign and refuses to start it, based on the guest's real memory and filesystem facts rather than on the commands alone.

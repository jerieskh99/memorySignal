Two gates that measured the wrong thing

An experiment that needed no new captures at all — 4.7 megabytes of existing trajectories and fifteen seconds of laptop time. It returned winner = null, and both reasons turned out to be defects in how the pass criteria were built.

The one idea to hold onto

When every option fails your test, suspect the test. Both failures here came from gates that were structurally incapable of measuring what they claimed — one blind to the variable it was scoring, one counting cells that could never have passed.

1
The first defect

A score that was blind to the thing it scored

The question was narrow and cheap: given the sampling rate from the previous entry, what window size and step should the analysis use to slice each trajectory? Twelve combinations, ninety trajectories, no capture required.

Before it ran, the plan written eight days earlier was already obsolete. It had proposed windows from 32 up to 512 samples. The actual trajectories held between 5 and 73 samples — so zero of ninety cells could accommodate even the smallest proposed window. The range was cut by a factor of eight, to 8 through 64.

Then the sweep ran and declared no winner for either workload. Every combination failed at least one gate. The first reason:

plan03_window_hop_proposal.html · defect 1

The gate scoring the bursty workload used a boundary-detection score. But the detector's function signature takes only the trajectory — no window, no step. So its score was identical across all twelve combinations for a given cell.

A gate meant to choose between twelve options was returning the same number for all twelve. It could not discriminate even in principle.

It was replaced with a measure that genuinely depends on window size — the sharpness of the dominant periodic peak, whose frequency resolution is set by the window length. The old score was kept, but demoted to a regression guard against the previous dataset.

Takeaway

Before trusting a gate to rank options, check that its output actually varies with the thing being ranked. A constant is not a criterion.

2
The second defect

A denominator full of impossible cells

The second gate required that a healthy fraction of cells yield a usable number of windows. It was failing everywhere — because its denominator counted every cell, including the short ones that hold five to fifteen samples and therefore cannot produce the required windows at any sensible setting.

Analogy

Judging a high-jump competition by the fraction of everyone in the stadium who cleared the bar. Most of them never attempted it. The ratio says nothing about the jumpers.

The fix restricts the denominator to cells that are physically eligible — those with enough samples to fit the window and step under test. With both gates repaired, the sweep returned an answer.

Cost of the whole experiment

Ninety trajectories, 4.7 MB on disk, about fifteen seconds of computation. Later, over the full dataset: 132 cells × 12 combinations = 1,584 rows in roughly thirty seconds. The expensive part of this research is capture; the analysis is nearly free, which is exactly why the later entries argue so hard about what to keep.

3
The result

One answer, arrived at from opposite directions

Both workload types converge on the same setting — a window of 8 samples with a step of 4 — and the interesting part is that they get there for incompatible reasons.

Steady workloads

Smallest is cleanest

Variability rises monotonically with window size — 0.037 at width 8, climbing to 0.868 at width 64. A long window swallows several complete cycles of the workload and smears them together.

Bursty workloads

Smallest that still resolves

Picked as the smallest window that still produces a strong periodic peak (8.87 dB) — among the few gates this family can reach at all.

Verdict

Universal, with one exception

Across all eleven workloads later, the same setting wins everywhere. One workload prefers a wider window, and that pick was flagged as fragile — only two cells in the dataset can even support it.

This setting is still what the whole thesis runs on. It is one of the few decisions from this period that has never been revisited.

The classification result

Feeding ten features from this sweep into a classifier gave a perfect score at telling bursty from steady — 1.000, every one of 118 cells correct. Identifying which of eleven workloads produced a trace was far weaker at 33%, but structured: the errors stay almost entirely inside a workload's own family. Two workloads that share a sweeping access pattern were mutually confused, which is the instrument behaving correctly.

Be honest about the limits

Recorded at the time: all four repeats came from the same host, the same guest, within a single day. Nothing here tests whether the result survives a different machine or a different week. And that perfect binary score is the subject of the next entry, where it does not survive contact with an audit.

4
The honest negative

The wall that was not the analyzer's fault

One gate fails universally — zero of twelve combinations, for every bursty workload, in both datasets. And unlike the two defects above, this one is real.

To resolve a rhythm, the analysis window has to span at least two of its cycles. The encryption phase being detected lasts about twenty seconds, so the window needs to cover forty — which at the chosen sampling rate means at least eighty samples. The longest bursty trajectory in the entire dataset is sixty-seven.

The alternative was written down explicitly: relax the coverage requirement from 2.0 to 0.5, lower the detection bar, and the gate passes. That option was recorded and rejected.

plan03_overview.html

"The team's recommendation is to retain the honest best-feasible label."

The result is reported as: the rhythm is present and measurable, but under-sampled at this capture rate. That phrasing survives every later review intact — it is one of the few claims in the project that no audit has dented.

5
The tension

Moving a threshold after seeing the data

When the analysis was re-run over the full eleven-workload dataset, two problems appeared at once. The first was mechanical: the code recognised only two workload names as substrings, so on the new dataset every workload classified as unknown and no criterion was computed at all.

The second was substantive. The thresholds had been calibrated against two probes — one clean burst and one clean cycle, with a baseline variability of 0.022 that the write-up itself calls "toy-clean". Applied to eleven real sustained workloads, they rejected essentially everything.

The write-up argues at length that this is recalibration, not relaxation — a genuine stall still trips the gate. But the project's own founding principles include the line "no metric is ever picked post-hoc to make the result look good." The document resolves the tension by not hiding behind it:

The admission, verbatim

"The thresholds were post-data-tuned against v3's per-workload medians, as recorded in the D-83 commit."

Be honest about the limits

Stating a violation clearly is better than concealing it, but it is not the same as avoiding it. There was no held-out set here — the same cells that calibrated the thresholds were then certified by them. This pattern recurs three more times in the next entry, and by the fourth occurrence it has consequences.

One commit from this week deserves a mention on its own: "rewrite all prose in academic style (no caveman)." The research notes had been written in clipped fragments for speed; turning them into a document that someone else could read was a deliberate, separate act of work.

Glossary

Hover any dotted term for its definition.

Analysis window
A run of consecutive snapshots analysed as one unit. Its length sets the frequency resolution — which is exactly why a score that ignores it cannot rank window sizes.
Gate
A pass/fail criterion applied to a cell or a workload. This entry is about two of them that were structurally incapable of measuring what they claimed.
Periodic peak strength
How sharply a repeating rhythm stands out above the noise floor, measured in decibels. Unlike the score it replaced, it genuinely depends on the window length.

The night the headline collapsed

A change-point detector, built and corrected five times between 00:55 and 02:30. Then, the same day, a review that took the project's best number apart and found it was a single feature wearing a disguise.

The one idea to hold onto

The perfect score was real, reproducible, and meaningless. One feature was the label, rewritten — and finding that out is the most valuable thing this project produced all month.

1
The build

Ninety-five minutes, and why the detector has one knob

The previous entry left a placeholder: a boundary detector that ignored the window size. Replacing it properly meant choosing from five candidates — a classical cumulative-sum test, two Bayesian and penalty-based segmenters, a robust derivative rule, and a small neural network.

The selection argument is the sharpest piece of reasoning in the period, and it is about budget rather than sophistication:

plan04_segmenter_proposal.html · dispute 1 · unanimous

"With approximately 20 marker-rich cells per duration stratum, any detector with more than one knob is over-parameterised."

The Bayesian option needs a prior, the penalty method needs a cost model, the network needs a training set that does not exist. The winner has exactly one tunable number.

What shipped: a two-sided cumulative-sum test running on the rolling mean, with the scale estimated robustly so a few large spikes cannot inflate it, and one sensitivity knob whose default is the familiar two-standard-deviation rule. Around 370 lines, no file access, six pure functions, and a 736-line test suite.

The entire arc — proposal, implementation, quality review, and five corrective decisions — is committed between 00:55 and 02:30 on a single night.

2
The blocker

A schema mismatch that faked every pass

The quality review found the detector itself was correct. What was wrong sat between the detector and the thing that judged it.

The driver wrote its gate results as top-level fields. The validator looked for them nested inside a sub-object, under different names. The chain:

the nested dictionary did not exist
  → the lookup returned None
    → bool(None) is False
      → so the validator took its fallback path
        → and the fallback path was the "not applicable" branch
          → which counts as a PASS

Every one of 132 cells appeared to clear the gate. Every bursty workload had in fact failed it. Once the schema was fixed, the honest figure was 60 of 132.

Takeaway

A missing value and a passing value were indistinguishable to the validator. This is the same defect as Entry 6's cell that "failed for having succeeded quietly" — the identical confusion between absent and fine, pointed the opposite way, and this time it manufactured a perfect score instead of destroying a good one.

Two smaller ones, same review

The exported helper used the previous time step where the actual implementation used the current one — the detector was right, but its own public function disagreed with it. And the implemented gates were quietly looser than the written specification: a median threshold where the spec required a median and a proportion.

3
The real problem

Sixty-three hours, and then the real problem

With the gates honest, the detector still scored zero. The first cause was mundane and fixable: a 63-hour wall-clock skew between the log the workload wrote and the log the capture wrote. The matcher that aligned them by absolute time collapsed every truth marker onto the first snapshot. Aligning them relatively instead — first marker to first snapshot, spacing preserved — made markers land in sensible places.

And the score stayed at zero. Across every setting tried.

Inspecting individual cells showed why. One trace with 66 samples had 13 declared truth markers and the detector emitted none. Another with 93 samples had 2 markers and 3 detections, none of which overlapped.

plan04_overview.html §10

"The detector and the markers were not failing to find the same events; they were finding different kinds of event."

Phase markers describe events inside the workload — a file processed, a phase begun. The detector finds shifts in the level of memory activity. For a workload that encrypts steadily, those two definitions of "event" almost never coincide.

In plain terms

The ground truth and the measurement were never measuring the same thing. No amount of tuning reconciles them, because there is nothing wrong with either one.

4
The walk-back

The retreat, in five steps

Faced with a ground truth that could not be used, the gate was progressively redefined until something passed. Each step is individually defensible. Read together, they are the most instructive sequence in this diary.

StepWhat changedResult
D-84Fix the schema faking every pass; tighten gates to spec132/132 → 60/132
D-85Work around the 63-hour clock skewmarkers land, score still 0.0
D-86Abandon ground truth. A cell now passes if the detector emits between 1 and 20 boundariesbursty 0/5 → 3/5
D-87Give bursty workloads a knob 20× more sensitive than steady ones96/132 cells
D-88Lower the pass floor from 0.80 to 0.705/5 bursty, 120/132, "10 of 11 workloads pass"

The justification for the last one is that two workloads sat at exactly 0.75, and the 0.80 floor had been calibrated before the gate's meaning changed at D-86 — so it was measuring something else by then. That is a real argument. It is also the fourth threshold moved after seeing the data in two entries.

Be honest about the limits

The published headline became "10 of 11 workloads pass segmentation." What that sentence actually certifies, after D-86, is "the detector emitted somewhere between 1 and 20 boundaries" — a claim about liveness, not about phases. The next concept is what happened when someone checked whether even that much was true.

One gate was honestly labelled a placeholder

A fifth gate returns true for everything — a hardcoded placeholder, because the scores it would compare against are all zero. This is disclosed in the overview document. It is also, elsewhere, reported as "11/11 pass" — a discrepancy that is still open.

5
The audit

A hundred percent from one leaked number

Later the same day, the results were put through a structured internal review — a device this project had been using since the previous month, in which the work is argued over by a set of named roles rather than people. The documents say so plainly: the exchanges are reconstructions, and every number cited comes from files on disk. The critiques are staged; the ablations they produced, and the retractions, are real.

The first blocking objection was aimed straight at the perfect binary classification score from Entry 7:

reviewer_memo.html · finding B-1 · blocking

"A RandomForest reporting a [[54,0],[0,64]] confusion matrix on a one-number-per-snapshot signal is a red flag, not a triumph."

An ablation was written and run. It confirmed the objection completely.

Features usedAccuracy
All ten1.000
One feature — coverage_ratio — alone1.000
Drop the three suspect features0.644
Mean and spread of the raw signal0.576
Always guess the larger class0.542

That one feature takes the value 0.1333 for every steady cell and 0.200 for every bursty one. Zero variation within either group. It is not a measurement of the workload — it is the label, arithmetically re-encoded. Two further features leak the same way through their absence: one is undefined for all bursty cells, the other for all steady ones, so which one is missing tells you the answer.

The line from the research log

"So the model was rediscovering our labelling rule, not memory behavior. The hundred percent is feature construction."

A second round, 46 minutes later, stopped arguing about method and instead re-derived every printed number from the raw files. Its opening verdict on whether the portfolio's figures matched the data: "It mostly is not." Nine numeric corrections followed, including that the memory-copy cost had been quoted at its distribution minimum (0.75 s) when the median was 7.06 s, and that a claimed storage contribution had never been exercised at all — the relevant setting was on in 125 of 128 cells.

Three follow-up scripts closed the remaining questions against existing data, and all three came back negative:

Significance

The 0.644 residual is not distinguishable from guessing (p = 0.119). A bootstrap over the 33 genuinely independent groups puts its confidence interval at [0.541, 0.746] — the lower bound sits below the baseline.

Generalisation

Held out an entire unseen workload: 0.466 — worse than always guessing. The leaky version stays at exactly 1.000 in every fold, confirming the leaked feature is a per-family constant.

And the detector from earlier in this entry was run against nulls — shuffled versions of its own inputs, and pure noise matched for length and spread:

real bursty cells                 0.850
same data, time-shuffled          0.850   gap: 0.000
pure noise, matched statistics    0.850   gap: 0.000

The output file flags itself: band_near_unfalsifiable: true. The gate cannot tell a workload from its own shuffled noise, because what it actually certifies is that the trajectory is not flat.

6
The salvage

What survived

Losing every headline in a day sounds like a catastrophe. The position taken in the documents is the opposite, and it is correct:

Takeaway

"The negatives are the contribution." These are not failures to bury — several are the most publishable findings in the project.

What came through the review intact:

Claims that held
  • The cost model — and it got stronger. The correction turned "the snapshot dominates" from an assertion into a measured 81% of the cycle.
  • The coverage limit from Entry 7 — attacked on degrees-of-freedom grounds and it held. Restricted to the cells with the most windows, the peak strength rises from 5.78 to 6.92 dB.
  • The leakage finding itself — the second round only sharpened why it is true.
  • Marker non-observability — the negative from concept 3. Structurally unavailable, not merely low.
  • Identity does not survive the coarsening — 33% on an eleven-way task, with errors confined within families. Never challenged.
  • The central thesis — one deliberately impoverished number carries a coarse, weak hint of workload family and nothing finer.
Be honest about the limits

Two of the fourteen quantities audited checked out exactly as printed. That is the real ratio, and it is why the next entry's habit — chase the number you do not believe — is the one that pays off.

One loop from this day was never closed. The papers were frozen on 1 June; the documents that state the classification result still describe it as a genuine 100%, and the word "leakage" does not appear in them. The retraction lives in one directory and the claim lives in another.

Open, not done

Bringing those documents into line with their own audit is outstanding work, not a finished decision. It is listed here so the gap is visible rather than quietly inherited by whatever gets written next.

Glossary

Hover any dotted term for its definition.

CUSUM
A change-point detector that accumulates how far the signal has drifted from its recent average, and fires when the running total crosses a threshold. Chosen because it has exactly one tunable knob.
Phase marker
A timestamp the workload itself emits to say "a phase started here" — the intended ground truth. The finding of this entry is that it describes a different kind of event than the detector measures.
Leakage
When a feature encodes the answer rather than measuring the thing. Here one feature was a constant per class, so a model using it was reading the label, not the memory.
Held-out workload
Training with one entire workload removed, then testing on it. The honest test of whether a result generalises to something never seen — as opposed to merely to a new repeat of something familiar.

1
The gap between reasoning and evidence

The claim nobody tested

Entry 1 laid out why one complex number per page made sense — a size and a direction, fused so every downstream transform gets both for free. That argument is geometric, and it's sound as far as it goes. What it is not, anywhere in the thesis, is measured.

Claimed, never checked

There is no ablation anywhere in the thesis comparing the full complex representation against a magnitude-only version of the same pipeline.1 The single most consequential design decision in the whole project — fuse two numbers into one, or keep them separate — is asserted and never tested.

There is a second, quieter gap in the same place. The angle is built by stretching a distance that only ever runs from 0 to 1 around a full turn of the circle — which means its two extremes land on the same angle. Nothing in the thesis discusses that. What it costs, measured on real data, is Entry 12.

And there is a third gap, which is different in kind because it was known at the time. The thesis says the wavelet transform runs on the delta's magnitude and phase. The code that produced the results runs it on the real and imaginary parts instead — a different decomposition of the same complex signal, giving different coefficients and therefore different features. Both paths exist in the source. Only one was wired to the configuration that ran.

Sitting between them, in the function itself:

#TODO MAKE IT ALSO FOR MAGNITUDE PHASE!!!!!!!!!!!!!!
Eleven exclamation marks

This is not a defect that slipped through unnoticed. It is a note to self, in the author's own hand, saying the magnitude/phase route was the one he meant to take and hadn't finished. The results were produced on the other branch and written up as though they had been produced on this one.

Traced: wavelet_analysis_features_memmapped.py, inside process_wavelet_2d. With the driver's defaults (--exp-decay --parallel) the dispatch routes through …_overlap_parallel → …_decay_features_single_batch → is_mag=False. The magnitude branch is reachable only with decay switched off.

Takeaway

The most useful artifact in a codebase is often the comment where someone told themselves the truth. An urgent-sounding TODO is a dated record of what the author knew was unfinished — and it outlives the intention that wrote it.

Why this belongs here, not as a footnote

Every result in the rest of this entry rests on that representation. If a result looks strong, it is worth remembering that the foundation under it was never independently checked — which is exactly the habit the rest of this diary spends two years building tools for.

2
Under the hood

A detector chosen by elimination

The full pipeline: pause the guest, dump memory, compute per-page differences in Rust, assemble a page-by-time matrix, tile it into overlapping blocks, push each block through the four lenses from Entry 1, reduce to energy and shape statistics, compress the dimensions, and hand the result to a model.

The model is a single autoencoder, and the reason is stated without ceremony: "the lack of labelled memory data limited the use of supervised methods."1 No labelled corpus existed, so the only move available was a one-class model — learn what normal looks like, and flag whatever deviates, without ever being shown an example of the other class. Trained only on normal blocks, the autoencoder's reconstruction error becomes the anomaly score — large when a block looks unfamiliar, small when it looks like training data.

Two machines were tested. The first ran simulated ransomware in a continuous loop. The second ran a real cryptominer, sampled across three clean phases and three infected ones, deliberately spanning two restarts — a built-in stress test for whether "normal" holds still.

The habit paying off

The lens introduced almost as an afterthought — cepstrum — reached 100% across every clean phase, unaffected by block size or by either reboot. The lens the thesis was originally titled after, wavelets, placed second. That only happened because nothing was committed to in advance.

The result

The score he didn't believe

The ransomware machine's numbers were spectacular and worthless:

Machine 1 — ransomwareOn clean dataOn infected data
Accuracy0.00%100%
False positives100%—

The model was calling everything anomalous. That scores perfectly on infected data and catastrophically on clean data — the two numbers are the same failure, read from opposite sides. And the thesis says so, in its own results chapter, without being asked:

thesis §8.1, p. 93

"However, this performance is too good to be true and likely indicates a bias in the model."

This reflex reappears sixteen months later, almost word for word, aimed at a second perfect score that turns out to be one leaked feature. See Entry 8.

Takeaway

A perfect score is a symptom, not a triumph. It shows up here first, unprompted, in a results chapter that had no obligation to say it.

4
The real failure mode

It doesn't fear malware. It fears reboots.

The cryptominer machine's numbers are the ones the thesis actually rests on, and they degrade in a specific place: every method except cepstrum falls off sharply after the second restart.

Why this matters more than it looks

A detector whose baseline shifts on an ordinary restart cannot be deployed, and the problem is not in the model — it is that "normal" was assumed to be a stable thing that could be learned once. Two years later the project is still working on that exact assumption, and it is most of why later effort goes into controlled, repeatable capture rather than better classifiers.

5
The unwritten limitations

What wasn't written down

The thesis states its limitations honestly: not enough clean data, a bias toward calling things anomalous, the scattering transform needing work. Those are real. A harder set was never stated, and this is where a private diary earns its keep.2

n is one

Two machines, one piece of malware each. Machine 1 is unusable. Every reported success rests on a single virtual machine running a single cryptominer.

The labels aren't real

Accuracy on an infected batch assumes every block in it is anomalous. A miner touches a small fraction of two gigabytes; most blocks in an "infected" batch are almost certainly normal.

The infection is confounded

Malware is introduced by starting another process. Nothing separates the signature of mining from the signature of one more program existing.

The threshold sets its own floor

Flagging anything above the 95th percentile of training error guarantees that ~5% of clean blocks get flagged, by construction, before any test data is seen.

And one that became the next two years of work

The pipeline pauses a machine, dumps two gigabytes, computes, resumes. Nothing verifies that snapshots are evenly spaced in time — yet every one of the four lenses in this entry assumes exactly that. Part Two is what happens when someone finally checks.

None of this makes the thesis bad work. It was graded 10.0 and nominated for the highest distinction available. What it makes it is a result standing on ground nobody had surveyed — and the same person who built it is the one who went back to survey it.

Glossary

Hover any dotted term for its definition.

Autoencoder
A model trained to compress its input and rebuild it. Train it only on normal data and it rebuilds normal things well and unfamiliar things badly, so the size of the reconstruction error becomes an anomaly score. Chosen here because no labelled data existed.
Reconstruction error
How far the autoencoder's rebuilt version of a block differs from the real one. Small error means the block looked like the normal data it was trained on; large error is the anomaly signal.
Ablation
A test that removes one part of a method to see whether the result survives without it — here, comparing the full complex representation against a version with only the magnitude. The comparison this thesis never ran.
One-class model
A model trained on examples of only one category — here, "normal" — because no labelled examples of the other category exist. It learns what normal looks like and flags anything unlike it, rather than learning to tell two known classes apart.

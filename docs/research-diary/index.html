Research diary · 2024–2026

Reading Memory

Two years of building an instrument that watches a virtual machine's memory and tries to say what the machine is doing — without reading a single system call. It began as a detector that worked. This is the diary of everything that happened after refusing to believe it.

How to read this. Each entry is one stretch of work: what the question was, what got built, what happened, and what it forced next. The colour of each card is not decoration — violet marks the entries where something was built, amber where something was found to be broken, and teal where something was measured.

Every number here comes from a file, a commit, or a measurement I ran. Each entry's sources are listed at its foot and marked traced (I opened it or ran it myself) or inferred (assembled, and the document does not say so). Anything designed but not yet built carries a dashed amber box, so a plan is never mistaken for a result.

This is a private working record. It is deliberately harder on the work than any paper would be, including on the parts that were graded well.
